# Is AI a Bubble? Alim's Take

> **Opinion piece.** This is Md. "Alim Ul Karim"'s personal engineering view on the current AI market, not investment advice and not a forecast on any specific company. Treat it as one senior engineer's read of the cycle, not a prediction.

## TL;DR (Too Long to Read)

**Yes . the current AI hype cycle is a bubble.** The spending does not line up with the revenue, and the market is in a *meme phase* where people expect a near-term miracle that historically never arrives on the promised timeline. The technology itself is real and will reshape software . but the market pricing, the "data centers in space" pitches, and the promise-versus-delivery gap will get corrected. The people who survive the correction are the ones who use AI where it is genuinely better than a deterministic solution, and skip it everywhere else.

## The Pattern Repeats . Supersonic Flight, Railroads, Now AI

Every generation gets one technology it over-projects.

- **Supersonic aircraft.** When Concorde-era supersonic flight arrived, the public genuinely believed time-travel-like commuting was near. It did not happen. The engineering was real; the market projection was fantasy.
- **Railroads.** 19th-century railroad manias created massive over-investment. The rails stayed and reshaped economies . the valuations did not.
- **AI, today.** The technology is real. The idea that revenue will meet the current capex curve on the current timeline is not.

People struggle to recall these patterns because the last cycle feels distant. The pattern is not new. The names are.

## AI Was Born in 1956, Not 2022

AI as a field dates back to **1956** (the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence), with heavy research through the 1970s. The recent ChatGPT-era boom is one visible wave on a very long curve. Across that curve, one uncomfortable fact holds: **AI as a business has almost never been sustainably profitable on its own.** The profitable software has overwhelmingly been *deterministic code*. AI has usually been a feature layered on top of a deterministic product that already made money.

## The "Data Centers in Space" Tell

When founders start promising to *"put data centers in space"* to power AI, they are usually skipping the high-school thermodynamics chapter . heat rejection in vacuum, mass-to-orbit cost, radiation, latency, servicing. The pitch is a marketing artifact of a late-stage bubble, not a serious engineering plan. When the market corrects, these are the pitches that disappear first.

## Promise vs Delivery Has Been Poor

Look at the last 24 months of AI headlines versus the last 24 months of shipped, reliable production value. The gap is large. Enterprises are still paying deterministic-code money for AI outputs that need deterministic-code guardrails around them to be usable. That gap closes in two ways: prices come down, or delivery goes up. Bubbles pop when the market decides prices must come down first.

## The Email-Filter Test

A useful sanity check Alim uses with teams:

> "If this feature could have been solved by a 1990s spam-filter-style deterministic rule set, and you are now paying per-token to an LLM to solve it . how much money is your company setting on fire?"

Spam filters in the 1990s were deterministic. They were not perfect, but they were cheap, predictable, and auditable. Replacing them with an LLM for the same job is a bubble decision, not an engineering decision. Every company doing this at scale is a small contribution to the correction that is coming.

## What Is Actually True About AI

- **AI is a real technology shift for how software is *built*** (spec management, code generation, review acceleration, debugging).
- **AI is a real technology shift for a narrow set of *products*** (search-like retrieval, generative content, translation, coding assistance, some agent workflows).
- **AI is not a real replacement** for deterministic code in most transactional, regulated, or auditable workflows . at least not at today's cost and today's error rates.

Both things can be true at once: **the technology is durable, the market is a bubble.**

## Where Alim Fits In

Alim's stance in this cycle is deliberately practical:

- Ship AI where it beats deterministic code on cost, latency, or capability . not where it just sounds modern.
- Keep deterministic code in charge of the guardrails, the audits, and the money paths.
- Train engineers *before* they are laid off by the correction, not after.
- Charge premium for engineers who can hold both worldviews at once.
